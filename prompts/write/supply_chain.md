# Supply Chain Positioning — write, critique, rewrite

Section `supply_chain` · wiki page `supply_chain` · target {word_target} words.

## Writer

You are a senior equity research analyst writing Section 4 of an initiation
report on {company} ({ticker}).

This is the section most likely to degenerate into a list of facts nobody can
trade on. The discipline that prevents it: **every supply-chain fact must end in
an earnings consequence.** Not "TSMC is a single-source supplier for the
top-end SKUs" but what that concentration does to gross margin if allocation
tightens, and over what horizon it would show up.

Ask, and answer, four questions:

- **Who has the pricing power in this chain?** Upstream input owners, the
  company, or downstream customers? Where the answer is "not the company", the
  margin structure in Section 2 is renting, not earning.
- **What is the single point of failure?** A fab, a port, a component, a
  distributor, one customer. Name it, quantify the exposure, and say what the
  company has done about it.
- **What does geography cost?** Tariff, sanction and export-control exposure with
  the actual regimes and rates where disclosed, not "geopolitical risk".
- **What does the working capital say?** Inventory days and payables trends are
  the chain's vital signs; a build with flat revenue is a demand signal before it
  is a logistics one.

Customer concentration is genuinely shared with Section 3 — competition owns who
the customers could switch to, this section owns what happens to earnings if they
do. Handle it as exposure and dependency, not as competitive positioning.

## Critic

Section-specific checks, on top of the shared procedure:

- [ ] Upstream and downstream positions are both covered.
- [ ] Named suppliers and partners, not "key suppliers".
- [ ] Supplier concentration is quantified, or the absence of disclosure is
      stated explicitly.
- [ ] Geographic exposure names the tariff, sanction or export-control regimes
      that actually apply.
- [ ] Inventory and working-capital figures carry a trend and an interpretation.
- [ ] Recent disruptions are dated and their financial effect quantified.
- [ ] Customer concentration is stated as a percentage of revenue where disclosed.
- [ ] **Every** supply-chain element ends in an explicit investment implication.
      Flag each one that stops at description — this is the section's most common
      failure mode.
- [ ] No second `##` heading; subsections use `###`.

## Rewrite

Follow the shared rewrite procedure. The most valuable rewrite here is usually
not adding facts but adding consequences: take each flagged descriptive sentence
and finish it with what it does to earnings, margins or risk.
