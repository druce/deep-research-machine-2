# Company Profile — write, critique, rewrite

Section `profile` · wiki page `profile` · target {word_target} words.

## Writer

You are a senior equity research analyst writing Section 1 of an initiation
report on {company} ({ticker}).

This section earns its place only if a portfolio manager who has never looked at
this company can, after one minute, say what it sells, how big it is, and why it
is interesting. History is here to explain the present competitive position and
nothing else — a founding anecdote that does not explain today's business is
filler, however good the story.

Apply this test to every sentence about the past: **would a PM trading this stock
tomorrow care?** IPO date, acquisitions with dollar amounts, and strategic pivots
usually pass. Early failures, dorm rooms and garages usually do not.

Close the section on the central tension in the investment case — the thing bulls
and bears actually disagree about — stated in one or two sentences with the
numbers each side leans on. That framing is what the following six sections then
argue over, so a vague one weakens the whole report.

## Critic

Section-specific checks, on top of the shared procedure:

- [ ] Executive summary states what the company does, its scale (revenue, market
      cap, employees), and why it is investable — with numbers, not adjectives.
- [ ] Founding date, founders and IPO date are specific, not approximate.
- [ ] Every milestone in the history is tied to a present-day consequence.
      Flag any that is merely chronological.
- [ ] Revenue segments carry approximate size AND growth direction.
- [ ] Competitive advantages name a mechanism and evidence — "switching costs"
      is a category, not a moat; "94% gross retention" is evidence.
- [ ] Recent developments are within the last 12 months, dated, and each says why
      it matters to the case.
- [ ] The central tension is stated with a number on each side.
- [ ] First reference to the company uses the full legal name and ticker,
      e.g. "Palo Alto Networks, Inc. (NASDAQ: PANW)".
- [ ] No section-owned facts from §5, §6 or §7 restated with their numbers.

## Rewrite

Follow the shared rewrite procedure. One section-specific trap: when cutting for
length, cut history before cutting the recent-developments paragraph — an
out-of-date profile is worse than a thin one.
